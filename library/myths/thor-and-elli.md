# Thor Wrestles Old Age

Thor, the strongest of the gods, once travelled into Jotunheim, the land of the giants, with Loki and two human servants. After many days they came to a fortress so tall that they had to lean back until their necks cracked to see the top of it. The gate was shut, but Thor squeezed between the bars, and they walked into a great hall where giants sat on benches, and on the high seat sat their king, Utgard-Loki.

The king looked down at Thor and smiled. "Is this little fellow the famous Thor? No one stays among us who isn't master of some skill. What can you do?"

So the contests began, and they went badly. Loki raced a giant at eating and lost; his rival ate the meat, the bones and the trough. Thor's servant, the fastest runner among men, raced a slim young giant called Hugi and lost three times. Then Thor was handed a drinking horn and told that a good drinker empties it in one draught. He drank until his eyes watered, three long pulls, and the level barely dropped. He was told to lift the king's grey cat, and managed only to raise one paw off the floor.

By now Thor was red with rage. "Let someone wrestle me," he said.

The king looked around the hall as if embarrassed for him. "Everyone here would think it beneath them to wrestle you. But call in my old nurse, Elli. She has thrown men who looked stronger than you."

An old woman came in, bent and small. Thor went at her with all his strength, and the harder he pulled, the firmer she stood. Then she began to push, and slowly, steadily, she brought the thunder god down onto one knee. The king called an end to it.

In the morning Utgard-Loki walked his guests out of the fortress, and at the edge of his land he told them the truth. He had deceived them with illusions from the start. Loki's rival was wildfire, which eats everything. The runner was Hugi, Thought, and nothing is faster than thought. The horn's end had been set in the sea, and Thor had drunk so deeply that the tide went out; that is why the sea has tides. The cat was the World Serpent, which circles the earth, and Thor had lifted it almost to the sky.

"And Elli," the giant said, "was Old Age. There has never been anyone, and never will be, whom old age does not bring down in the end. That you only went down on one knee has frightened us all."

Thor raised his hammer, but the giant and his fortress were gone, and there was only a wide green plain.
