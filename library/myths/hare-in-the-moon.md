# The Hare in the Moon

Long ago, in a forest near a river, four friends lived together: a hare, an otter, a jackal and a monkey. The hare was the wisest of them, and in the evenings he would teach the others. "Be generous," he told them. "Keep the holy days. If anyone comes to you in need, give them something from your own store."

One such holy day was coming, and the hare said, "Tomorrow, if a beggar comes, let's each give him food from what we've gathered."

In the morning the otter went down to the river and found seven red fish that a fisherman had buried in the sand. He called out three times to ask whose they were, heard nothing, and took them home. The jackal found a lizard, a pot of curds and some meat in a field-watcher's hut, and took those. The monkey climbed a tree and gathered mangoes. Each laid their food aside and thought, when a beggar comes, I'll give this.

The hare had nothing. He ate grass, and you can't offer a guest grass. He lay in his thicket and thought, and then he thought, if someone comes, I will give him myself.

High above, the throne of Sakka, king of the gods, grew warm, as it did whenever someone on earth made a great resolve. Sakka looked down, saw the hare, and decided to test him. He took the shape of an old Brahmin, a travelling holy man, and went to the four friends one by one.

The otter offered his fish. The jackal offered his meat and curds. The monkey offered cool water and mangoes. The old man thanked each of them and said he would come back later. Then he came to the hare.

"I have no rice or beans or meat," the hare said. "But gather wood and make a fire, and I will give you something you have never been given."

The old man built a fire. When it was burning well, the hare shook himself three times, so that no small creature living in his fur would be hurt, and leapt into the flames with his whole heart, the way a swan settles onto a lotus pool.

The fire did not burn him. It felt as cool as snow. "What is this?" said the hare.

"I am Sakka," said the old man, "and I came to test you. Your generosity will be known for as long as this age of the world lasts." And he squeezed a mountain in his hand, took its juice, and with it drew the shape of a hare across the face of the moon.

It is still there. In India, in China, in Japan, in Korea, people look up at the full moon and see, instead of a man's face, a hare.
