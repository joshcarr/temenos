# Inanna's Descent

Inanna, Queen of Heaven, goddess of love and war and the morning star, set her ear to the Great Below. Her sister Ereshkigal ruled there, in the land of the dead, and Ereshkigal's husband had just died. Inanna decided to go down.

She knew the danger. Before she left, she told her faithful servant Ninshubur: "If I haven't returned in three days, beat the drum for me, go to the gods, and don't let them leave me there." Then she dressed for the journey in all her power. She put on her crown, her lapis beads, a double strand of beads at her breast, her breastplate called "Come, man, come," her gold ring, her measuring rod and line, and her royal robe. And she went to the outer gate of the underworld and knocked.

Ereshkigal, hearing who it was, bit her lip in rage. "Bolt the seven gates," she told her gatekeeper. "Open them one at a time. At each one, let her enter, and take something from her."

At the first gate, the gatekeeper took her crown. "What is this?" said Inanna. "Quiet, Inanna. The ways of the underworld are perfect. They may not be questioned." At the second gate, her small lapis beads. At the third, the double strand at her breast. At the fourth, the breastplate. At the fifth, the gold ring. At the sixth, the rod and line. At the seventh gate, her royal robe.

She came before her sister naked and bowed low. The seven judges of the underworld fixed their eyes on her, the eyes of death, and spoke against her, and she was turned into a corpse. Her body was hung on a hook on the wall. There it hung for three days and three nights.

On the fourth day, Ninshubur beat the drum and went from god to god. Two refused. The third, Enki, god of wisdom and water, was troubled. From the dirt under his fingernails he made two tiny creatures, neither male nor female, and sent them down with the food and water of life. They slipped through the gates like flies and found Ereshkigal groaning, in labour or in grief. When she moaned, "Oh, my inside," they moaned with her. When she moaned, "Oh, my outside," they moaned with her. No one had ever grieved with her before. She was so moved that she offered them any gift, and they asked for the body on the hook.

They sprinkled it with the food and water of life, and Inanna rose. But no one leaves the underworld without leaving someone behind, and demons followed her up to take her place. The story of whom she gave them is another story.
