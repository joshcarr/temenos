# Hotei and the Sack

In China, a thousand years ago, a monk walked the roads with a big round belly, a staff and a cloth sack over his shoulder. People called him Budai, which means cloth sack. Later, in Japan, they called him Hotei. He had no temple and no fixed place to sleep. He slept wherever night found him, in a doorway, under a bridge, in the snow, and the stories say the snow did not settle on him.

He begged for anything at all. Rice, fish, a scrap of meat, a cake. Some of it he ate on the spot. The rest went into the sack. And then, wherever he went, children followed him, because out of the sack came sweets and toys and odd small treasures, and somehow there was always another one.

People could never decide if he was a fool or a sage. He laughed at almost everything. He told fortunes by the weather; if he slept outside in his sandals it would rain, if he wore wooden clogs and lay down on a bridge it would be fine. Farmers watched him and planned their harvests by him.

There is a story that a teacher of meditation met him on the road and asked, "What is the meaning of Zen?" Hotei did not answer. He just let the sack drop to the ground.

"And what is the realisation of Zen?" the teacher asked.

Hotei bent down, picked the sack up again, slung it over his shoulder and walked on, laughing.

Another time a monk walking ahead of him heard a voice behind say, "Give me a coin." The monk turned around, and Hotei held out his hand: "Give me a coin." It was the whole teaching. Ask for something, receive it gladly, give it away, ask again.

When he was dying he sat on a rock outside a temple and spoke a short verse. He said he was Maitreya, the Buddha who is to come, and that people saw him every day and did not know him. Then he died sitting. After that people began to see him, round and laughing, in carvings and on shop counters, and to rub his belly for luck.

In Japan he became one of the Seven Lucky Gods, who sail in on their treasure ship at New Year. Of all of them he is the one who looks most like he has just been told a joke. The sack is still full. Nobody has ever found the bottom of it, perhaps because he never stopped giving it away.
