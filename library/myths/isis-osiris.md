# Isis Gathers Osiris

Osiris was the first king of Egypt, and a good one. He taught people to plant grain and tend vines, gave them laws, and travelled the world teaching the same things to other nations. His wife and sister Isis ruled while he was away. His brother Set watched all of it and hated him.

When Osiris came home, Set gave a banquet in his honour. At the height of it he had a wonderful chest carried in, cedar inlaid with ebony and gold, and announced that whoever fitted inside it exactly could keep it. The guests tried one after another, laughing, and none of them fitted, because Set had secretly had it built to his brother's measurements. Then Osiris lay down in it. At once Set's men slammed the lid, nailed it shut, sealed it with molten lead and threw it into the Nile.

The river carried it to the sea, and the sea carried it to Byblos, on the coast of Lebanon, where it lodged in a tamarisk tree. The tree grew around the chest and closed over it, and it grew so tall and fragrant that the king of Byblos had it cut down and made into a pillar for his palace.

Isis cut her hair and put on mourning and searched everywhere. At last she came to Byblos, sat by a well as a stranger, and became nurse to the king's baby. In time she told the queen who she was, and asked for the pillar, and cut her husband out of it and carried him home.

But while she was away from the chest, Set went hunting by moonlight in the marshes and found it. He tore the body into fourteen pieces and scattered them up and down the whole length of Egypt.

So Isis began again. She made a boat of papyrus reeds and poled through the marshes, searching. Wherever she found a piece, people later built a shrine, which is why so many towns in Egypt claimed to have the grave of Osiris. She found every part but one, which a fish had swallowed, and she made that part herself.

With her sister Nephthys and the jackal god Anubis she laid the pieces together and wrapped them in linen, and that was the first mummy. Then Isis became a kite, the hawk of the river, and beat her wings over him, and the wind of her wings was breath. Osiris lived long enough for them to conceive a son, Horus, who would one day fight Set for the throne.

Osiris did not come back to the world above. He went down and became lord of the dead, the green-faced king who judges every soul. But each year, when the Nile flooded and the grain came up from the black mud, the Egyptians said it was Osiris rising.
