# Durga and the Buffalo Demon

Mahishasura, the buffalo demon, had done years of fierce austerities, and at last the creator god Brahma granted him a boon. He asked that no man and no god could ever kill him. He did not bother to mention women; it did not occur to him that a woman could be a threat.

With that protection, he made war on heaven. He could change shape at will, from buffalo to lion to elephant to man and back, and his armies were numberless. For a hundred years the gods fought him and lost. At last Indra, king of the gods, was driven from his throne, and the demon sat on it, and the gods wandered the earth like beggars.

They went to Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva and told them everything. As the great gods listened, their anger grew so hot that it poured out of their faces as light. From Vishnu came a blaze, and from Shiva, and from Brahma; then from Indra and all the other gods, each giving out a flame of their own fury. The fires ran together into one great mountain of light, so bright it filled the sky. And out of the light stepped a woman.

She had many arms, eighteen in some tellings, ten in others, and each god gave her a weapon from their own hand. Shiva gave her a trident, Vishnu a discus, Indra a thunderbolt, Varuna a conch shell, Agni a spear. The Himalaya gave her a lion to ride. Her name was Durga, which means "hard to approach," and she laughed, and her laughter shook the three worlds.

Mahishasura's generals rode out against her with their armies. She cut through them like a field of grass, and her lion fed on the demons, and her breath became fresh troops. Then the buffalo demon himself came to fight her. He charged in his buffalo form and she caught him with a noose; he became a lion and she cut off its head; he became a man with a sword and she cut him down; he became an elephant and she cut off its trunk; he became a buffalo again and shook the mountains with his horns.

Durga paused and drank. Then she leapt onto his back, pinned his neck under her foot, and struck him with her spear. As he tried to come out of the buffalo's mouth in his true form, she cut off his head.

The gods sang her praises. Every autumn, in the festival of Durga Puja, she is honoured across India, and especially in Bengal, as the power that rises when every other power has failed.
