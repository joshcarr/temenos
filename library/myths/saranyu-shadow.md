# Saranyu and the Shadow

Saranyu was the daughter of Tvashtr, the craftsman of the gods, who forges their weapons and shapes every living body in the womb. She was given in marriage to Surya, the Sun. It was a great match. It was also impossible.

Surya blazed. He could not do otherwise. His radiance was so fierce that Saranyu could not look at him, could hardly stand to be in the same room. She closed her eyes when he came near. She bore him children, Manu, the first man, and the twins Yama and Yami, but she was exhausted by the glare of him, day after day without shade.

So she made a plan. She took her own shadow, and out of it she made a woman exactly like herself, and named her Chhaya, which means shadow. "Stay here in my place," Saranyu told her. "Look after my children. Be his wife. Don't tell him." Then she turned herself into a mare and galloped away north, to a cool country of long grass, where she grazed alone for years and did penance in the quiet.

Surya never noticed. The shadow was perfect. Chhaya bore him children of her own, among them Shani, the slow dark planet the West calls Saturn. But a shadow is not a mother, and she favoured her own children over Saranyu's. One day young Yama lost his temper with her and raised his foot to kick her, and Chhaya cursed him. Yama ran to his father. Surya thought: no mother would ever curse her own child like that. He looked at his wife properly for the first time in years, and saw that she was only a shadow.

He went to his father-in-law, Tvashtr, in a fury. Where was his wife? Tvashtr told him the truth, and then told him something harder: she had left because no one could live beside so much light.

So Surya let the craftsman put him on his lathe. Tvashtr turned him and shaved away part of his blazing, until he was bright enough to light the world and gentle enough to be looked at. The shavings that fell from the lathe were so powerful that Tvashtr forged them into weapons for the gods: Vishnu's discus, Shiva's trident.

Then Surya took the form of a stallion and went north to find the mare. They met in the long grass, and they were together again, and from that meeting were born the Ashvins, the twin horsemen of the dawn, who are the physicians of the gods.

The Sun got his wife back by letting himself be made smaller. In the old hymns the twin healers still ride out ahead of him every morning, just before he rises.
