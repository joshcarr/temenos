# The Drowned City of Ys

On the coast of Brittany, in the bay of Douarnenez, there was once a city called Ys, the most beautiful in all of Cornouaille. King Gradlon built it for his daughter, Dahut, who loved the sea, and he built it below the level of the tide. A great dyke held the water back, with bronze gates that let ships in at low tide and kept the ocean out at high. There was one golden key to the gates, and the king wore it on a chain round his neck, even when he slept.

Ys grew rich. Its towers were white, its streets were full of music, its ships came home heavy. The monks who later wrote the story down said it also grew wicked, and that Dahut was the worst of all: proud, pleasure-loving, faithful to the old sea gods rather than to the new church. In some versions she took a new lover every night. In some she was half a fairy already, a daughter of the sea.

One night a stranger came to the palace, a man dressed in red, handsome and charming, and Dahut fell for him. He asked her for one proof of her love. He wanted the key.

She crept into her father's room while he slept and lifted the chain from his neck. She gave the key to her lover. And he, or she herself in some tellings, went down to the dyke in the dark and opened the gates at the top of the tide.

The sea came in. It ran down the streets of Ys and filled the squares and climbed the stairs of the towers. King Gradlon woke to the sound of water and found the city drowning. He leapt onto his horse, Morvarc'h, the horse of the sea, and pulled Dahut up behind him, and they galloped for the shore with the waves at the horse's heels.

But the sea was gaining. A voice, a saint's voice, called out to the king to throw down the demon behind him. Gradlon hesitated. Then Dahut fell or was pushed into the water, and at once the waves stopped. The king reached land alone and founded a new capital at Quimper.

Dahut became a mermaid. Fishermen say they still see her combing her hair on the rocks, and that she drowns sailors because she misses her city.

And Ys is still down there, perfect under the water. On calm days, people used to say, you could hear its church bells ringing under the bay. The Bretons also had a saying. When Paris, *Par-Is*, the city equal to Ys, is swallowed in its turn, Ys will rise again.
