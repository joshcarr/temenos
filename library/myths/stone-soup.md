# Stone Soup

Three soldiers were walking home from a war. They had no money and they had not eaten for two days. Ahead of them was a village, and as they came up the road they could see the villagers hurrying about: the bread disappearing from windowsills, the sacks of grain going down into cellars, the cows being led round the back of barns. It had been a hard year. Nobody in that village was going to feed three hungry soldiers.

The soldiers knocked at every door and heard every excuse. The harvest was poor. The children were sick. There was nothing in the house at all.

So the soldiers stood in the middle of the square and said, loudly, "Well. Then we'll have to make stone soup."

The villagers came out to look. The soldiers asked to borrow the biggest pot in the village, and someone, curious, brought it. They filled it with water from the well and built a fire under it. Then one of them took three smooth round stones out of his pack, wiped them carefully on his sleeve and dropped them in.

The water heated. The soldiers stirred. One of them tasted it and smacked his lips. "Wonderful. Of course, stone soup is always better with a little salt and pepper."

A woman ran home and came back with salt and pepper.

"Excellent," said the soldier, tasting again. "The last time we made this, we had a couple of carrots. But never mind."

A man remembered he might have a couple of carrots.

"And an onion makes all the difference," said the second soldier, to no one in particular. An onion appeared. Then cabbage. Then a few potatoes, a handful of barley, a ham bone, a jug of milk. Someone brought bread, and someone else, a little shamefaced, brought beer. Tables were dragged into the square. That night the whole village sat down together and ate the best soup any of them could remember, and everyone agreed it was a marvel what could be made from stones.

In Portugal it is a friar who makes the soup, and in Sweden and Norway a tramp who makes it out of a nail. The French version is often a joke at the villagers' expense. But it is also a story about how plenty happens. Nobody in the village had enough for a feast. Everybody had a little. It only needed someone to set out the pot and act as if there was enough to go round.
