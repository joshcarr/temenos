# Anansi Buys the Sky God's Stories

In the beginning all the stories in the world belonged to Nyame, the Sky God. He kept them in a box beside his throne, and nobody on earth had any stories at all. Kwaku Anansi, the spider, decided he wanted them. So he spun a thread up into the sky and climbed it and asked the Sky God what the price would be.

Nyame laughed. Great kings had come to buy those stories and gone away, because the price was too high. "I will sell them," he said, "for four things. Bring me Onini the python, Osebo the leopard, Mmoboro the hornets, and Mmoatia, the fairy whom no one sees." He was sure the spider would never come back.

Anansi went home and thought. Then he cut a long palm branch and some creeper and walked to where the python lived, arguing loudly with himself. "It's longer than he is. No, it isn't. Yes, it is." Onini put his head out. "What are you arguing about?" "My wife says you're shorter than this palm branch. I say you're longer." "Lay it down beside me," said the python, "and we'll see." Onini stretched himself along the branch, but he couldn't reach both ends at once. "Let me tie you at the front so you don't slip," said Anansi, and he tied the head, and then the middle, and then the tail, and the python was caught.

For the leopard he dug a pit on the path and covered it with branches. Osebo fell in at night. In the morning Anansi said, "Poor friend. Let me help you out," bent a tree down over the pit, and tied a rope to it. When the leopard climbed the rope, Anansi let the tree go, and Osebo was hanging in the air, caught.

For the hornets, he filled a calabash with water and poured some over a banana leaf held over his head, and some over the hornets' nest. "It's raining," he called to them. "Come into my dry calabash." In they flew, and he plugged the hole with grass.

For the fairy, he carved a wooden doll, covered it with sticky gum, and set it under the odum tree with a bowl of mashed yams in its lap. Mmoatia came and ate the yams. "Thank you," she said to the doll. The doll said nothing. "Answer me," she said, and slapped it, and her hand stuck, and then her other hand, and then she was caught.

Anansi carried all four up the thread to the sky. The court fell silent. Nyame looked at them, and then he stood and said: "Great kings could not pay my price, but Kwaku Anansi has paid it. From today my stories belong to Anansi, and they will be called spider stories." So they are, across West Africa and the Caribbean, to this day.
