# Chang'e Flies to the Moon

Once there were ten suns. They were the children of the god of the eastern sky, and they were supposed to cross the heavens one at a time, a different one each day. But one morning all ten came up together for the fun of it. The rivers dried. The crops burned in the fields. The rocks split, and people hid in caves and died there of thirst.

The great archer Hou Yi was sent to deal with them. He climbed a mountain, drew his red bow, and shot the suns out of the sky one after another. Each one fell as a three-legged golden crow. When nine had fallen, the people begged him to stop and leave one, so there would still be day. He lowered his bow. The world was saved, but the god of the eastern sky had lost nine children, and Hou Yi and his wife, Chang'e, who had lived in heaven, were sent to live on earth as mortals.

Hou Yi did not want to die, and he did not want Chang'e to die. He travelled to the far west, to the Kunlun mountains, where the Queen Mother of the West keeps her garden, and she gave him an elixir of immortality. There was just enough for two people to live for ever on earth, or for one person to rise to heaven as a god. He took it home and hid it, meaning to share it with his wife on an auspicious day.

What happened next is told many ways. In some versions Chang'e drank the whole thing out of curiosity or longing for heaven. In the version told most often today, one of Hou Yi's students, a man called Fengmeng, broke into the house while the archer was out hunting and demanded the elixir. Chang'e could not fight him off. So she drank it herself.

At once she felt herself grow light. Her feet left the floor. She rose through the roof and over the trees and kept rising, and because she wanted to stay as near as she could to her husband, she stopped at the Moon.

It is cold there. She lives in the Palace of Great Cold with a jade hare, who sits pounding herbs in a mortar for medicine, and a woodcutter named Wu Gang, who chops at a cassia tree that heals over every time he cuts it.

When Hou Yi came home and found her gone, he looked up and saw a figure on the Moon that looked like his wife. He set out a table in the garden with the fruits and cakes she loved. People still do the same on the Mid-Autumn night, when the Moon is fullest: they set out mooncakes, and look up, and think of whoever they can't be with.
