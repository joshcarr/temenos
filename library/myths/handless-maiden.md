# The Girl Without Hands

A poor miller was cutting wood in the forest when an old man he had never seen stepped out from behind a tree. "Why go to the trouble of chopping wood?" said the old man. "I'll make you rich, if you promise me what's standing behind your mill." The miller thought: that's only my old apple tree. So he agreed. When he got home, his wife told him that chests of gold had appeared all over the house. And then she went pale, because their daughter had been standing behind the mill, sweeping the yard.

The old man was the devil. When he came to collect, the girl had washed herself clean and drawn a circle of chalk around herself, and he couldn't come near her. He came again, and she had wept onto her hands so that they were clean, and again he couldn't touch her. So the devil told the miller: cut off her hands, or I'll take you instead. The miller, terrified, went to his daughter. "Do with me as you like," she said. "I'm your child." He cut off her hands. She wept onto the stumps, and they were so clean the devil had to give up.

Her father offered her a life of comfort. She refused. She had her arms bound behind her and walked out into the world.

She came at night to a king's garden, where pears hung heavy on the trees, and she was starving. An angel came and dried up a moat so she could cross, and she ate a pear straight from the branch with her mouth. The king found her there, heard her story, and loved her. He married her and had silver hands made for her.

But while the king was away at war, she gave birth to a son, and the devil switched the messages between the palace and the king, so that the king's loving letters arrived as orders to kill her. The king's old mother couldn't do it. She cut out the tongue and eyes of a deer as false proof, bound the baby to the young queen's back, and sent them away.

She went into a great wild forest, and an angel led her to a small house with a sign that said: here everyone lives free. She lived there seven years, caring for her son. And because she was so good and so faithful, her hands grew back, first as a child's hands, then as a grown woman's, the old stumps gone.

The king came home, learned the truth, and searched for her for seven years without eating or drinking. At last he found the little house in the forest, and his wife, and his son, and the hands he didn't recognise. They went home together and had a second wedding.
