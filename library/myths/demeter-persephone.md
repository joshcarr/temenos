# Demeter Searches for Her Daughter

Demeter, goddess of the grain, had one daughter, Kore, whom she loved more than the harvest. One spring morning the girl was gathering flowers with her friends in a meadow: crocus, iris, hyacinth, roses. Then she saw a flower she had never seen before, a narcissus with a hundred heads, so sweet that heaven smiled at it. She reached for it with both hands. The earth split open at her feet, and out of the crack came the lord of the dead in his chariot of black horses. He caught her up and drove back down, and the ground closed over them. She screamed for her father, Zeus, but Zeus had agreed to the marriage, and he did not come.

Her mother heard the last of the scream echo off the mountains, and a sharp grief took her heart. She tore the veil from her hair, put on a dark cloak and went searching. For nine days she walked the earth with a burning torch in each hand, and did not eat, or drink, or bathe. On the tenth day, Hecate, who hears things at crossroads, came to her with a torch of her own and said, "I heard her, but I didn't see who took her." Together they went to Helios, the Sun, who sees everything, and he told her plainly: Zeus gave her to his brother Hades. He's not such a bad son-in-law.

Demeter did not go back to Olympus. She disguised herself as an old woman and sat down by a well in the town of Eleusis, and took work as a nurse in a king's house, and for a while she tried to make the king's baby son immortal by laying him in the fire at night. His mother saw and screamed, and the goddess rose to her full height, furious and shining, and demanded a temple. When it was built she sat in it alone and grieved.

And while she grieved, nothing grew. The oxen dragged the ploughs through the fields for nothing; the seed lay in the ground and would not come up. People began to starve, and the gods began to miss their offerings. Zeus sent messenger after messenger to Demeter, with gifts and pleas. She sent them all back. She would not let the earth bear fruit, she said, until she saw her daughter with her own eyes.

At last Zeus sent Hermes down to the underworld to bring the girl home. Hades agreed. And the rest of the story belongs to the daughter, who by then had a new name: Persephone.
