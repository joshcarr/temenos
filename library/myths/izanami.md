# Izanagi in the Land of Yomi

In the beginning, the Japanese chronicles say, two gods stood on the floating bridge of heaven. They were Izanagi, he who invites, and Izanami, she who invites. They stirred the brine below with a jewelled spear, and the drops that fell from its tip became the first island. They went down and married there, and Izanami gave birth to the islands of Japan, and then to the gods of the wind, the trees, the mountains and the sea.

The last child she bore was the god of fire, and he burned her as he was born. She sickened and died and went down to Yomi, the land of the dead.

Izanagi could not bear it. He followed her down into the dark, all the way to the doors of the palace of the dead, and called to her to come home: the world they were making was not finished.

Izanami answered from behind the door. "You came too late. I have already eaten the food of this place. But I will ask the gods of Yomi if I may go back. Wait here. Whatever you do, do not look at me."

He waited. She was gone a long time. At last he could not stand it. He broke a tooth off the comb in his hair and lit it as a torch and went in.

He saw her. She was lying in the dark, rotting. Maggots swarmed over her body, and eight gods of thunder had been born out of her flesh and sat on her head, her breasts, her belly, her limbs.

Izanagi turned and ran.

"You have shamed me," Izanami cried, and sent the hags of Yomi after him. He threw down his black headdress and it turned into grapes, and the hags stopped to eat them. He threw down his comb and it sprouted into bamboo shoots, and they stopped again. Then she sent the thunder gods and an army of the dead. At the slope that leads up out of Yomi he found a peach tree, threw three peaches at them, and they fled.

Last of all Izanami came after him herself. Izanagi rolled a great boulder across the pass, so big a thousand men could hardly move it, and they spoke to each other through the stone.

"Every day I will strangle a thousand of the people of your land," she said.

"Then every day I will cause fifteen hundred to be born," he answered.

That is why people die, the story says, and why more are born. Izanagi went down to a river to wash off the uncleanness of the underworld. When he washed his left eye, the sun goddess Amaterasu was born. When he washed his right eye, the Moon god. When he washed his nose, the storm god. What he had brought back from the dark was the light.
