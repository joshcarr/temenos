# Philoctetes on Lemnos

When Heracles lay dying on his pyre on Mount Oeta, nobody would light it. They could not bear to burn a man still breathing. Only young Philoctetes stepped forward with the torch, and for that hard kindness Heracles gave him his bow, the one that never missed, and the arrows dipped in the Hydra's poison.

Years later Philoctetes sailed with the Greek fleet for Troy. On the way the ships stopped at a small island to make an offering, and as he walked through the sacred grove a snake bit him on the foot. The wound would not close. It swelled and blackened and wept, and it smelled so foul that the men could not eat near him, and in his worst hours he screamed so loudly that the priests could not finish their prayers.

Odysseus advised the commanders to leave him. One night, while Philoctetes slept, they put him ashore on the empty island of Lemnos with his bow and a few rags, and sailed on.

He lived there ten years. He found a cave with two mouths, so he could have sun in winter and a breeze in summer. He shot birds with Heracles' bow and dragged himself to fetch them. He packed his foot with leaves. He talked to no one, and the thing he lived on, besides the birds, was his anger at the men who had left him.

Meanwhile the war went badly. At last a captured Trojan prophet told the Greeks that Troy would never fall without the bow of Heracles. So Odysseus sailed back to Lemnos, with Achilles' young son Neoptolemus, and a plan: the boy would win the wounded man's trust with a lie and walk away with the bow.

Neoptolemus did it. He listened to Philoctetes' story, he held the bow while the man collapsed in a fit of pain, and then he could not go on. He gave the bow back and told him the truth. It was a choice that might have cost the war.

In Sophocles' play it is the ghost of Heracles who finally appears and tells Philoctetes to go to Troy, where he will be healed. He went. The son of Asclepius cleaned and closed the wound, and Philoctetes fought, and Troy fell.

The Greeks needed exactly the man they could not stand to be near. The wound and the gift had come to him on the same journey, and nobody could have the one without putting up with the other.
