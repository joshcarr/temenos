# Zhuangzi's Butterfly

Zhuangzi was a Chinese philosopher who lived more than two thousand years ago, in the time of the Warring States. He was a Taoist, a follower of the Way, the nameless pattern that runs through all things. The book that carries his name is full of talking trees, giant birds, a cook who carves oxen without ever blunting his knife, and a great many jokes. It is also where the most famous dream in China was written down.

Once, Zhuangzi dreamed that he was a butterfly. He fluttered here and there, happy with himself, doing as he pleased. He didn't know he was Zhuangzi. He was simply a butterfly, flitting about in the warm air, going from flower to flower, delighted.

Then suddenly he woke up, and there he was, solid and unmistakably Zhuangzi, lying on his mat.

But he didn't know whether he was Zhuangzi who had dreamed he was a butterfly, or a butterfly now dreaming that it was Zhuangzi.

He noted that between Zhuangzi and a butterfly there must be some distinction. This, he said, is what is called the transformation of things.

That is the whole story. It is only a few lines long in the original, and people have been arguing about it ever since. Some read it as a question about reality: how do you know you're awake now? Others read it as an invitation to stop holding so tightly to one fixed self. The butterfly was happy because it wasn't worrying about being a butterfly. Zhuangzi, waking, is at once a little more solid and a little less sure.

Elsewhere in the same book Zhuangzi tells of a man who dreams he's drinking wine at a feast and wakes up weeping, and another who dreams he's weeping and wakes up to go hunting. While we dream, he says, we don't know it's a dream; we may even interpret a dream inside the dream. Only after we wake do we know it was a dream. And perhaps some day there will be a great awakening, and we will know that this, too, was a great dream. Meanwhile the fools think they're awake, and are busy deciding who is a prince and who is a herdsman.

When his wife died, a friend found Zhuangzi sitting on the ground, drumming on a pot and singing. The friend was shocked. Zhuangzi said that he had wept at first, but then he thought about how she had come out of nothing and gone back into it, like the passing of the seasons. And he stopped.
