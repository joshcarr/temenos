# The Nigredo

The old alchemists said the Great Work begins in lead. Lead was Saturn's metal: heavy, grey, dull, poisonous, the least promising thing in the workshop. And yet this was the matter they started with, because, they said, everything needed for gold was hidden inside it. *Visit the interior of the earth*, ran one of their mottoes; *by purifying you will find the hidden stone.*

So here is how an old alchemist might have explained it to a new apprentice.

First you find the *prima materia*, the first matter. Nobody will tell you what it is. The books call it by a hundred names. They say it is found on dunghills, that children play with it in the street, that everyone has it and no one values it. Put it in the sealed glass vessel. Set the vessel in the furnace. Keep the fire low and steady, like the warmth of a hen on her eggs. Then wait.

What happens next will frighten you. The matter in the glass begins to darken. It swells and sweats and gives off fumes. Then it turns black, blacker than anything you have seen, and it begins to rot. It stinks. The books call this the *nigredo*, the blackening. They call it the raven's head, and the black sun, and the death of the king. In the old pictures, the king and queen lie together in a coffin, and they are one body, and they are dead.

The apprentice will want to stop here. He will think he has ruined everything. He will want to open the vessel or turn up the fire to force the work along. If he does, the work is lost.

The master says: do neither. This is the work. Nothing can be made new that has not first been taken apart. Keep the fire steady. Keep the vessel sealed. Watch it, and write down what you see.

In time, if you are patient, the black begins to lighten. The books say that a glitter of many colours spreads across it, like a peacock's tail, and then it whitens, the *albedo*, as if washed. Later it yellows, and at last, they say, it reddens, and that is the stone.

Whether any alchemist ever made gold, nobody knows. But in the twentieth century the psychologist C. G. Jung read their books and decided they had been describing something else as well: what happens in a person when an old self comes apart. Depression, mourning, the dark nights when nothing seems to work. The alchemists' advice for that stage still stands. Don't run from the blackening. Keep the vessel closed and the fire low. Stay with it until it changes colour.
