# Chiron and the Poisoned Arrow

Most centaurs, the people who were men above the waist and horses below, were wild. They drank too much, fought at weddings, and carried off other men's wives. Chiron was different. His father was the Titan Kronos, who had taken the form of a horse to hide from his wife, and his mother was the sea nymph Philyra, who was so horrified at the sight of her baby that she begged the gods to change her into something else, and became a linden tree. So Chiron was abandoned at birth. Apollo and Artemis found him and raised him, and they taught him everything they knew: music, medicine, prophecy, archery, hunting.

He lived in a cave on Mount Pelion and became the greatest teacher in Greece. Heroes were sent to him as boys. He taught Asclepius, who became the god of medicine. He taught Jason, who sailed for the Golden Fleece, and Achilles, whom he fed on the marrow of lions and bears, and taught to play the lyre and heal wounds as well as make them. He was known as the wisest and most just of the centaurs.

His friend Heracles came to visit the centaurs once, and one of them, Pholus, opened a jar of wine for him that belonged to all the centaurs. The smell drew the others, and they came wild and drunk and armed with rocks and pine trees, and there was a fight. Heracles shot his arrows into them. His arrows had been dipped in the blood of the Hydra, the many-headed serpent he had killed, and the poison was incurable. The centaurs fled, some of them to Chiron's cave. Heracles shot after them. One arrow went through a centaur's arm and struck Chiron in the knee.

Heracles, horrified, ran to him, pulled out the arrow, and put on a medicine Chiron himself had taught him. It did nothing. The Hydra's poison was stronger than any medicine. And Chiron could not die, because he was immortal. He was left with a wound that would never heal and a pain that would never end, and no way out.

He went on teaching. He went on healing others. He lived that way a long time.

In the end, the stories say, he found a way to lay down his immortality. Prometheus was chained to his rock in the Caucasus, and Zeus had said he could only be freed if an immortal would agree to die in his place. Chiron agreed. He gave up his life so that Prometheus could be released, and he died, and Zeus set him in the sky as the constellation Centaurus.
