# Orpheus in the Underworld

Orpheus was the greatest singer who ever lived. When he played the lyre, the wild animals came out of the woods to lie at his feet, trees pulled up their roots to follow him, and rivers stopped to listen. He married Eurydice, and they loved each other. But on their wedding day, or soon after, Eurydice was walking in a meadow when she stepped on a snake, and it bit her ankle, and she died.

Orpheus sang his grief until the mountains wept, and then he did what no living man had done. He went down into the underworld to bring her back.

He took the path through a cave at Taenarum, down into the dark, and he played. Charon the ferryman, who never carried the living, carried him across the river Styx. Cerberus, the three-headed dog, lay down and let him pass. In the depths of Tartarus, where the damned suffer, the punishments stopped while he played. Tantalus stopped reaching for the water. Sisyphus sat down on his rock. The Furies wept, for the first time.

He came before the throne of Hades and Persephone and sang to them of his love and his loss. He wasn't asking for a gift, he sang, only a loan: everyone comes to you in the end; let her live out her years and she'll be yours again. Persephone was moved, and so, for once, was Hades.

They called Eurydice. She came from among the new shades, still limping from her wound. They could go, Hades said. But there was one condition: Orpheus must walk ahead of her, all the way up to the light, and he must not turn to look at her until they had both left the underworld.

So they climbed, along the steep, dark, silent path. He could not hear her footsteps. Was she there? Had they tricked him? He came to the very edge of the light, and in fear, or in longing, he turned to look.

She was right behind him. He saw her for one moment, and she was already slipping back. He reached for her and caught only air. She didn't blame him, the poet Ovid says; what could she complain of, except that she had been loved? She said a last goodbye he could barely hear, and was gone.

Orpheus tried to go back, but Charon wouldn't take him again. For seven days he sat on the bank without eating. He wandered the world after that, singing only of her, until he died, torn apart by maenads who were angry that he'd refused them. His head floated down the river, still singing.
