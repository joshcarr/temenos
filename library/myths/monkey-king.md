# The Monkey King Turns Heaven Upside Down

On the Mountain of Flowers and Fruit there was a stone that had soaked up the light of heaven and earth since the world began. One day it split open and out came a stone egg, and out of the egg came a monkey. His eyes flashed two beams of golden light up to the palace of heaven, and the Jade Emperor himself looked down to see what the trouble was.

The monkey was bold from the start. He jumped through a waterfall and found a cave behind it, and the other monkeys made him their king. But he was troubled by one thing: one day he would die. So he crossed the sea and found a Taoist master, and studied with him until he had learned the seventy-two transformations and the cloud-somersault, which carried him a hundred and eight thousand miles in a single leap. His master named him Sun Wukong, Awakened to Emptiness.

Back home, Monkey went down to the palace of the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea and borrowed a weapon: the iron pillar that measured the depth of the ocean, which shrank on command to a needle he kept behind his ear. When death's messengers came for him one night, he followed them down to the underworld, found the ledger where every creature's lifespan was written, and took a brush and crossed out his own name, and the names of all the monkeys.

To keep him quiet, the Jade Emperor gave him a post: Keeper of the Heavenly Horses. When Monkey learned this was the lowest job in heaven, he went home in a rage and called himself the Great Sage Equal to Heaven. Heaven let him keep the title and set him to guard the garden of the peaches of immortality.

He ate the peaches. Then he gatecrashed the Queen Mother's banquet and drank the wine. Then he wandered into the laboratory of Laozi and ate all the pills of immortality as if they were roasted beans. Armies of heaven came after him and he beat them all. They put him in Laozi's furnace for forty-nine days, and he came out with eyes of fiery gold.

At last the Jade Emperor sent for the Buddha. "I'll make you a wager," the Buddha said. "If you can jump out of my right palm, the throne of heaven is yours."

Monkey laughed and leapt. He somersaulted to the very end of the world, where five pink pillars rose into the clouds. He wrote on one of them, *The Great Sage Equal to Heaven was here*, and peed at its foot for good measure, and leapt back.

The Buddha opened his hand. On his middle finger was the writing, and a faint smell of monkey. Then he turned his palm over, and it became a mountain, and Monkey was pinned under it for five hundred years, until a monk came by who needed someone clever for a long journey.
