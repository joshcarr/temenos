# Markandeya Inside the Dream of Vishnu

Between one world and the next, the Hindu stories say, there is nothing but water. The old universe has burned and drowned, the stars have gone out, and there is only a dark ocean without shores, and on it, floating, the great serpent Shesha, coiled into a bed. On the serpent Vishnu lies asleep. Shesha's thousand hoods spread over him like a canopy. Out of Vishnu's navel, in time, a lotus will grow, and in the lotus the god Brahma will open his eyes and begin to make a new world. But for now he sleeps, and the whole of everything is his dream.

There was once a sage called Markandeya, who had been granted a life so long that he lived on into that great night. He was wandering, as he always wandered, through forests and pilgrimage places, through holy rivers and cities, and he did not know that all of it was inside the body of the sleeping god.

One day, walking, he slipped. He fell out of the sleeper's mouth.

Suddenly he was in the dark water. There was no land, no sun, no sky. Cold black waves went on for ever. He had no idea where he was, and he was terribly afraid. He swam and swam. Then he saw, in the dark, a great sleeping shape on the water, shining faintly, like a mountain in a storm. He did not recognise it. How could he? He had spent his whole life inside it.

Then a vast hand took hold of him, and he was swallowed again, and he was back in the world he knew, the forests, the rivers, the pilgrims on the roads. He told himself it must have been a dream.

It happened a second time. This time, out in the dark water, he saw a banyan tree growing from nowhere, and on one of its leaves lay a small child, perfectly calm, sucking its toe. The child looked at the drowning sage with great kindness and said, "Come here, Markandeya, my child. Don't be afraid." Markandeya was offended at being called child by a baby. Then the child opened its mouth and breathed in, and Markandeya was drawn inside, and there again were all the worlds, the gods, the oceans, the cities, his own hermitage. He understood then that the baby was the sleeping god.

He spent many years inside, they say, and came out once more to sit and sing to the child on the leaf.

The story is told in the Mahabharata and the Puranas. The sage who has seen everything has never seen the sea he is floating in, and the dreamer turns out to be a child.
