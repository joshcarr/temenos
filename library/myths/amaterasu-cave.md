# Amaterasu in the Cave

Amaterasu, the sun goddess, ruled the Plain of High Heaven. Her brother Susanoo, god of storms, was wild and jealous. He broke down the walls between her rice fields, filled in the irrigation ditches, and defiled the hall where she tasted the first fruits of the harvest. She forgave him, again and again. Then one day, while she sat in her sacred weaving hall with her women, making garments for the gods, Susanoo tore a hole in the roof and threw down a piebald horse he had flayed. A weaving maiden was so frightened she struck herself with her shuttle and died.

Amaterasu had had enough. She went into the Rock Cave of Heaven, pushed the great stone door shut, and would not come out.

The world went dark. Night went on and on. Without the sun, the rice would not grow, and all kinds of trouble swarmed up out of the darkness like summer flies. The eight hundred myriad gods gathered by the dry riverbed of heaven to decide what to do, and the wisest of them, Omoikane, came up with a plan.

They brought roosters, the long-singing birds of the dawn, and set them crowing outside the cave. They made a great mirror and hung it on a sacred sakaki tree along with strings of jewels. And they turned a tub upside down in front of the door, and the goddess Ame-no-Uzume climbed up on it.

She began to dance. She stamped on the hollow tub until it boomed like a drum. She danced as if possessed, faster and wilder, until her robe came loose and slipped down, and she danced on, laughing, half-naked, and the whole assembly of the gods burst out laughing. The laughter shook the Plain of High Heaven.

Inside the cave, Amaterasu heard it. How could they be laughing, when the world was dark without her? She pushed the stone door open a crack and called out. Uzume answered: "We're celebrating, because a goddess even more glorious than you has come."

And as Amaterasu leaned out to look, the gods held up the mirror. She saw a dazzling light in it, her own, and was so astonished that she stepped out a little further to see. A strong god standing hidden beside the door took her hand and drew her out, and another stretched a sacred rope across the cave mouth behind her so she could not go back.

Light returned to heaven and earth. The mirror is said to be kept at the shrine at Ise to this day, one of the three sacred treasures of Japan.
