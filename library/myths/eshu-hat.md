# Eshu's Two-Coloured Hat

There were once two farmers who were the best of friends. They had grown up together, and when they were men they took land side by side, with only a narrow path between their fields. They worked in the mornings within shouting distance of each other and ate their midday meal together in the shade. Everyone in the village said there had never been such friends. They said so themselves, often, and they swore they would never quarrel.

Eshu heard them. Eshu is the guardian of crossroads, the messenger who carries offerings between people and the orisha, the gods. He knows every message has at least two sides, and he doesn't much like people who are too sure of anything, even friendship.

So he made himself a cap. One half of it was bright red and the other was black. Some tell it that the colours were white and red, or that the cap was split front and back. Either way, a man who saw it from one side would see only one colour. Eshu put it on, pulled his pipe from the back of his neck, and walked slowly down the path between the two fields, greeting both friends as he passed.

That afternoon, as they ate together, one friend said, "Did you see the stranger in the red cap this morning?" The other said, "You mean the black cap." "No, it was red. Bright red. I saw it clearly." "I was as close to him as you were. It was black."

They argued. Each knew what he had seen with his own eyes, and each thought the other was either blind or lying. Soon it wasn't about the hat any more; it was about which of them was a fool, and which was a liar, and every old slight they had ever swallowed came up. They shouted. They came to blows. The villagers dragged them to the king to settle it.

At the king's court, Eshu stepped forward and laughed. He showed them the cap, turning it slowly, red, then black, then red. "You are both right," he said. "And you are both wrong. I did it. Sowing argument is my greatest joy."

The friends stared at each other. They had each seen only half, and been perfectly certain of it.

The story is told across Yorubaland, and in Brazil and Cuba where the orisha travelled with the people who kept them. Eshu isn't evil in it. He's the one who reminds you there's another side of the hat, and that the other person may be standing on it.
