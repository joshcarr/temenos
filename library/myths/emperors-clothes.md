# The Emperor's New Clothes

There was once an emperor who loved new clothes so much that he spent all his money on them. He didn't care about his soldiers or the theatre or riding in the park, except as chances to show off a new outfit. He had a coat for every hour of the day.

One day two swindlers came to the city. They said they were weavers, and that they could weave the most beautiful cloth in the world. Not only were its colours and patterns uncommonly fine, but clothes made from it had a wonderful quality: they were invisible to anyone who was unfit for his office or unusually stupid.

"Those would be marvellous clothes," thought the emperor. "If I wore them, I could find out which men in my kingdom are unfit for their posts, and tell the clever from the stupid." He paid them a great deal of money in advance, and they set up two looms and pretended to work, though there was nothing on them at all. They asked for the finest silk and gold thread, put it in their own bags, and worked at the empty looms late into the night.

The emperor sent his honest old minister to see how the work was going. The old man stared at the empty looms. "Heaven help me," he thought, "I can't see anything. Am I stupid? Am I unfit for my office?" So he praised the pattern and the colours, and the swindlers described them to him in detail so he could repeat it to the emperor. A second official went, and did the same. Then the emperor went himself, with all his courtiers. He saw nothing. "Am I unfit to be emperor?" he thought. "That would be the worst thing that could happen." So he said, "It's very beautiful," and everyone agreed.

On the day of the great procession, the swindlers dressed him with great care, lifting each invisible garment and smoothing it. The chamberlains held up the invisible train. The emperor walked through the streets under a canopy, and all the people in the streets and at the windows cried, "How splendid the emperor's new clothes are! What a train! How well they fit!" Nobody would admit he could see nothing, because that would have meant he was stupid or unfit for his job.

"But he hasn't got anything on," said a little child.

"Listen to the innocent," said the child's father. And the whisper went through the crowd: he has nothing on, a child says he has nothing on. Until at last everyone was shouting it.

The emperor shivered, because he suspected they were right. But he thought, "I must go through with the procession." So he walked on more proudly than ever, and the chamberlains walked behind him, carrying the train that wasn't there.
