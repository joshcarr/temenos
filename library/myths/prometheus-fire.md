# Prometheus Steals Fire

Prometheus was a Titan, and his name means Forethought. When the gods made living creatures out of earth and fire deep in the ground, they gave Prometheus and his brother Epimetheus, Afterthought, the job of handing out gifts to them before they came up into the light. Epimetheus asked to do the handing out. He gave the animals everything: speed to some and strength to others, claws and horns, fur against the cold, wings, shells, burrows. Then Prometheus came to inspect the work and found that his brother had used up every gift, and there stood human beings, naked, barefoot, with no weapons and no bed.

So Prometheus decided to give them what the gods kept for themselves. He climbed to Olympus, or to the workshop of Hephaestus, the smith god, and took fire. He hid a glowing coal in the hollow stalk of a giant fennel plant, where it could smoulder without being seen, and he carried it down to earth and gave it to people. With fire came the other arts: cooking, metalwork, the working of clay, warmth in winter, light at night.

Some say there was an earlier trick. When gods and people met to settle which part of a sacrificed ox belonged to whom, Prometheus wrapped the good meat in the ox's stomach, where it looked unappetising, and wrapped the bones in shining fat. He let Zeus choose. Zeus chose the fat and found bones underneath, and that is why people burn bones for the gods and eat the meat themselves. Zeus had already taken fire away from humans in his anger at that trick. The fennel stalk brought it back.

Zeus's revenge was terrible. He had Prometheus carried to the Caucasus mountains and chained to a rock with bronze chains that could not be broken. Every day an eagle came and tore at his liver, and every night the liver grew back, so that the pain would never end. Prometheus knew it would happen. He was Forethought; he had done it anyway.

He hung there for generations. In Aeschylus's play *Prometheus Bound*, he refuses to beg, and refuses to tell Zeus a secret he knows about Zeus's own future. At last the hero Heracles, passing by, shot the eagle and broke the chains.

For the Greeks, Prometheus was the friend of humankind and the one who pays for it. For later writers, and for Richard Tarnas, who saw him in the planet Uranus, he became the patron of every sudden leap: the rebel, the inventor, the one who brings the fire down and doesn't ask permission.
