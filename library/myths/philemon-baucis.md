# Philemon and Baucis

In the hill country of Phrygia there is an oak and a linden tree growing side by side, with a low wall around them. This is how they came to be there.

Jupiter once came down to earth disguised as a mortal, with his son Mercury, who had put away his wings. They went from house to house in the region asking for a place to rest, and a thousand doors were shut in their faces. At last they came to a small cottage thatched with reeds and marsh-grass. An old woman called Baucis lived there with her husband Philemon, who was just as old. They had married young in that cottage and grown old in it together, and they had made their poverty bearable by admitting it and not minding.

When the strangers ducked under the low doorway, Philemon set out a bench and Baucis threw a rough blanket over it. She raked the ashes of yesterday's fire and blew on them with her thin breath until they caught, and fed them dry leaves and bark. Philemon brought in cabbage from the garden and took down a side of smoked pork from the rafters, and cut a small piece off it. They talked to their guests to pass the time while the meal cooked. The table had one short leg, and Baucis levelled it with a piece of broken pot.

They served olives, cherries preserved in wine lees, endive, radish, cheese, eggs roasted in the ashes, all on earthenware. The wine was young and poor. For dessert there were nuts, figs, dates, plums, apples and grapes, and a honeycomb in the middle of the table. And over it all were kind faces, and a welcome that was neither slow nor mean.

Then the old couple noticed that the wine bowl kept filling itself up. They were afraid, and began to apologise for the plain food, and went to catch their one goose to kill it for their guests. The goose ran between their legs, and the gods told them to let it be.

"We are gods," said Jupiter. "Your neighbours will be punished. You will be spared. Come with us up the mountain." From the slope, they looked back and saw the whole valley under a marsh, and only their cottage standing, and as they watched it turned into a temple with marble columns and a golden roof.

Jupiter asked what they wished for. They talked for a moment, and Philemon said: "Let us be priests of this temple. And since we have lived our lives together, let us die in the same hour, so that I never see her grave, nor she mine."

It was granted. When they were very old, standing on the temple steps, Baucis saw Philemon putting out leaves, and he saw her doing the same. They said goodbye to each other while they still could, and the bark closed over their faces. The story is told in Ovid's *Metamorphoses*.
