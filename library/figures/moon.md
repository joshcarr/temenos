# Moon: the Mother of Tides, the Night Gardener

## Core
Feeling, memory, the body's weather. What feeds and what needs feeding.

## Gift
Belonging, attunement, the capacity to be moved and to hold.

## Shadow
Moods that rule from underneath. Old hungers. Clinging. The child who still waits by the door.

## Jungian echo
The mother archetype, both the one who nourishes and the one who won't let go. The soul-image Jung called the anima, read here without his gender rules: the inner figure who carries feeling, whoever they turn out to be. In alchemy the Moon is silver and the *albedo*, the whitening, when things wash clean after the black.

## Myths
- hina-moon: Hina, worn out from beating tapa cloth all day for others, climbs to the Moon and stays (Polynesian)
- change-moon: Chang'e drinks the elixir and floats to the Moon, where only a hare keeps her company (Chinese)
- hare-in-the-moon: The hare who had nothing to offer a hungry traveller but itself, and was drawn on the Moon for it (Indian, Buddhist Jataka)
- selene-endymion: Selene visits the sleeping shepherd every night and never wakes him (Greek)
- demeter-persephone: Demeter searches the whole earth for her daughter, and nothing grows while she grieves (Greek)

## Images
A tide line of wrack. A bowl of milk. A well at night. A sleeping cat. Washing on a line. A hare. A shell held to the ear. A kitchen lamp left on.

## Readings

### pond-walker · synchronicity
At the pond the heron stood so long I stopped seeing it, and then it moved, and the water remembered it was there. The Moon is like that. She's always pulling the tide in you a little this way, a little that, and you only notice when something spills. Right now nothing needs fixing. Just notice what's rising and what's drawing back. And then, gently, the question: whose comfort have you been looking after, and when did you last let anyone look after yours?

### fairy-tale-reader · imaginal
In the tales, the youngest is always sent to the well, and something speaks up out of the water: a frog, a head, an old woman. The child who is polite to it gets gold; the one who sneers gets toads. This is not a lesson in manners. It's about how you treat what comes up from below: the mood you didn't order, the feeling that looks ugly. The Moon rules that well. So what came up, and were you polite to it? And what did you dream?

### howler · trickster
Holy the Moon, holy the dirty dishes she shines on, holy the laundry left in the washer overnight smelling of swamp, holy the leftover soup, holy the call to your mother you didn't make and holy the one you did! Holy the cat who wants in and out and in! The Moon is the goddess of all of it, the wet, the warm, the unfinished, the milk and the spilled milk. Make a list, a long ragged one, of everything that fed you. Leave nothing out for being small.

## Figure prompts

### synchronicity
- The Moon changes sign every two and a half days, and so do you. What mood has been passing through, and what did it want?
- Where is the tide in you right now: coming in, or going out?
- What did your body know before you did?

### imaginal
- A child is waiting up for you somewhere inside. What do they want to be told?
- The Night Gardener walks through your house with a watering can. What does she water, and what does she let go dry?
- Picture the kitchen you'd go to if you could go anywhere to be fed. Who's at the stove?

### trickster
- The dice came up Moon. Write the menu for a comforting meal, and give each dish a mood instead of a name.
- Describe your feelings as the weather report for a small coastal town.
- Which feeling are you calling "just tired"?

## Wild cards
- What do you still carry in your pockets from childhood, even if only in your head?
- Write a lullaby for the part of you that won't go to sleep. Four lines. It doesn't have to rhyme.
- If your mood were an animal curled up somewhere in the house, where would you find it?
- What do you need that you're a little embarrassed to need?
- Draw tonight's Moon. Then draw your own phase.
- Name a smell that takes you home. Which home?
