# Chiron: the Wounded Healer, the Centaur Teacher

## Core
The wound that teaches. Where the hurt becomes the gift.

## Gift
Compassion born of experience. Teaching. The ability to sit with someone else's pain.

## Shadow
The wound made into an identity. The helper who can't be helped. Shame kept hidden.

## Jungian echo
The wounded healer, an image Jung returned to often: the analyst's own wounds are what make the healing possible. Chiron is a centaur, half animal, half human, holding the split between body and mind that the work has to hold. Found only in 1977, orbiting between Saturn and Uranus: a bridge between the old order and the new.

## Myths
- chiron-arrow: Chiron, wisest of the centaurs and teacher of heroes, is struck by a poisoned arrow by accident and can neither die of it nor be cured (Greek)
- fisher-king: The Fisher King's wound won't heal, and the land lies waste until someone asks him the right question (Arthurian, Welsh and French)
- asclepius: Asclepius, taught by Chiron, heals so well that he raises the dead, and Zeus strikes him down (Greek)
- handless-maiden: A girl whose hands were cut off lives seven years in a forest, and her hands grow back as she cares for her child (German, Brothers Grimm)
- philoctetes: Philoctetes, bitten by a snake on the way to Troy, is left alone on an island with a wound that stinks and a bow the Greeks can't win without (Greek)

## Images
A scar gone silver. A cracked bowl mended with gold. A centaur at the edge of a wood. A herb growing in a cracked pot. A bridge between two banks. A bandage drying on a line. Firelight in a mountain cave. An arrow laid down.

## Readings

### old-man-at-the-lake · imaginal
Chiron taught the heroes medicine, music and the hunt, and then he was struck by a poisoned arrow that was not even meant for him. He could not die of it and he could not cure it. So he went on teaching. I have found it so with my patients, and with myself. The healer does not stand above the wound; he stands in it, and knows the way through because he is still walking it. Where is your old wound, the one that will not close? Ask it what it has taught you.

### grief-walker · synchronicity
There's a kind of hurt that doesn't finish. We're told it should, that there are stages and at the end a tidy room. That's a lie, and a cruel one. Some wounds are a life's companions, and they deserve to be walked with the way you'd walk with an old dog. Chiron knew. He never healed, and he became the best teacher there was. What have you been trying to get over that might be asking, instead, to be carried well?

### mythographer · trickster
In the Grail stories, the Fisher King sits with a wound that won't heal and the whole kingdom goes to dust around him. Knights ride through, admire the castle, eat the dinner and leave. All it would take is one question: what ails you? Parsifal fails to ask it the first time because he's been taught that good knights don't pry. Think of that: the cure held up by good manners. Whose wound have you been too polite to ask about, your own included?

### nun-at-the-sea · synchronicity
There's an old practice where you breathe in pain, as if drawing in dark smoke, and breathe out whatever relief you can find, as if sending cool light. It sounds backwards; we're trained to take in the good and push away the bad. But when you start with your own hurt, you find it isn't only yours. A great many people are sitting with this same ache right now. Chiron knew. Breathe in your old wound, for yourself and for everyone who shares it. Breathe out ease. Then write down who else carries it.

### mountain-hand · imaginal
Rolled an ankle on the scree once, twenty miles from the road. Nobody coming. You learn things. How to splint with a pack stay and a bandana. Which way to lean. That going slow is still going. Chiron is the old packer who taught the young ones all that, and he limped his whole life. Picture him on the trail ahead of you, favouring one leg, still making good time. What does he tell you about carrying your own old injury? Write it down plain.

### pond-walker · synchronicity
There's a heron at the pond with a bad wing. It can't quite fold it, and it stands a little crooked in the shallows. It still fishes. It fishes better than the others, I think, because it has learned to wait. I watch it most mornings. Chiron is like that bird: the hurt is part of the shape now, and the shape has its own grace. Where has an old hurt taught you to wait better? And the harder question: whom do you still refuse to let see the wing?

### fairy-tale-reader · trickster
Think of the girl without hands in the old story. Her own father cut them off to save his skin. She walks out into the world with her arms bound behind her, eats pears off the king's tree with her mouth, and after seven years in the forest her hands grow back. Nobody lectures her about forgiveness. She gets on with living, and the healing comes sideways. Where are you waiting for an apology before you'll live? And what did you dream?

## Figure prompts

### synchronicity
- Which old hurt has been stirring lately, and what stirred it?
- Where has your own pain made you useful to someone else?
- What do you know how to do because of what went wrong?

### imaginal
- A centaur waits at the edge of the wood with a bundle of herbs. Which one does he hand you, and for what?
- Your oldest wound has a voice. Let it speak for three lines without interruption.
- The Wounded Healer sits beside someone you love who is hurting. What does he do that you haven't tried?

### trickster
- The dice came up Chiron. Write a short job advert for the healer your old wound has turned you into.
- Draw a map of your scars and name each one like a town.
- Mend something on paper with gold: take a failure and rewrite it as a seam.

## Wild cards
- What question would you want someone to ask you, if they were brave enough?
- What have you been told to get over that you'd rather carry well?
- Where do you give the care you never received?
- Which part of you is half one thing and half another, like the centaur?
- Write what your wound would say if it were proud of you.
- What's the difference between a scar and a wound, for you?
