# Saturn: the Old King, the Stonemason

## Core
Limit, form, time. The weight that makes a thing real.

## Gift
Patience, craft, authority you earned, the bones of a life.

## Shadow
The inner critic. Fear dressed as prudence. The wall built too early.

## Jungian echo
The senex, the old man who knows how things are done and has forgotten why. Liz Greene's reading of Saturn as the place the shadow is most defended. Lead, the alchemical *prima materia*: the heavy, dark stuff the work has to start from.

## Myths
- kronos-swallows: Kronos swallowing his children for fear of being replaced (Greek)
- saturnalia: The Saturnalia, when the old king's golden age returned for a week and masters served their servants (Roman)
- nigredo: The *nigredo*, the blackening where the work begins (alchemical)
- thor-and-elli: Thor wrestles an old woman in the giant's hall and is brought to one knee; she was Old Age, whom no one throws (Norse)
- foolish-old-man: The Foolish Old Man who set out to move two mountains with a basket, one load at a time (Chinese)

## Images
A plumb line. A drystone wall. A sickle. A clock with no hands. A bar of lead. Winter trees. The bones of a house before the plaster. A ring heavy with keys.

## Readings
Offline readings, used when there's no network. The app puts one sentence about today's sky in front of them.

### mountain-hand · synchronicity
Stack wood the way the old ones did: bark up, split side down, a little air between. It isn't pretty and it isn't fast. It's the reason there's heat in February. Saturn is that kind of god. He doesn't care how the pile looks from the road. He cares whether it stands through the rain. Somewhere in you there's a pile half-stacked, and a voice saying you should be further along. Let the voice talk. Keep stacking. Then sit down with the page and count what's already dry.

### old-man-at-the-lake · imaginal
I have carved stone for many years, and it has taught me one thing: the stone decides. You come with a plan, the stone has a grain, and the grain wins. The old alchemists said the work begins in lead, in the blackening, when everything looks spoiled. They were not being gloomy. They meant that the heavy, dark matter is the only matter there is to work with. So don't ask what Saturn means. Ask what he wants, this grey old king. Then be quiet, and write down what he says.

### tavern-mystic · trickster
The Old King came into the tavern with his ledger, frowning at everyone's tab. I poured him a cup. He said he doesn't drink. I said, then hold it, your hands look cold. Friend, your inner accountant has kept books on you for years, and every page says "not enough." Buy him a drink. Ask to see the ledger. He may have been counting the wrong thing all along. Write down one entry he forgot to make.

### nun-at-the-sea · synchronicity
There's a moment, just before the old reaction, when you can feel the itch start. The jaw tightens. The story begins: this always happens, I should have known. Saturn is the teacher who keeps walking us back to that exact spot. Not to punish us; so we can practise staying. What if you didn't fix the discomfort, or explain it, or leave? Let it be as heavy as it is, and breathe. Write down where you felt the tightening today, and what happened when you stayed one breath longer.

### grief-walker · trickster
Now, the old ones had a name for the man who refused to get old: they called him a fool, and they fed him anyway. Saturn comes for all of us with his sickle and his ledger, and we've built a whole civilisation on pretending he isn't at the door. It's a bit funny, if you let it be. Here's a small defiance: count your grey hairs, or your aches, or your years, out loud, the way you'd count blessings. Then write down what time has given you that youth never could.

### engraver · synchronicity
The Old King builds with lead and hours, and every wall he raises is also a gate, if you walk far enough along it. Limit is the shape of the vessel; without it the wine is only a stain on the floor. Do not curse the mould that makes the bell. Somewhere this week you met a wall. Stand before it as a mason stands, measuring, not pleading. Write down what the wall is for, and what it keeps whole.

## Figure prompts

### synchronicity
- Saturn moves slowly across the sky, and some things in you are slow too. Which of your slownesses is wisdom, and which is fear?
- What in your life has taken exactly as long as it needed to?
- Where are you being asked to finish something rather than start something?

### imaginal
- If the stern one in you kept a workshop, what would be on the bench?
- The Old King sits at the end of your table. He hasn't spoken yet. Describe his hands.
- The Stonemason clears your desk. What does he keep, and what does he sweep into the bin?
- Let the inner critic write one sentence. Then let the Old King, who has actually built things, answer it.

### trickster
- The dice came up Saturn. Write for five minutes about nothing but a wall.
- Write your plans as a king's decree, with at least one absurd law.
- Saturn eats time. What did time eat lately, and was it hungry?

## Wild cards
- [trickster] Name one rule you follow that nobody gave you. Break it on paper.
- Draw the shape of your week as a building. Where are the load-bearing walls?
- Write a note to yourself from age ninety about something you're rushing.
- Which of your habits is a small stone wall? Who does it keep out, and who in?
- Write the word "no" ten times. Circle the one that sounds most like you.
- What in you is lead, and what might it be before it's gold?
