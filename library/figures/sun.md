# Sun: the King in the Sun, the Heart-Fire

## Core
Self, vitality, the centre that radiates. What you're for.

## Gift
Warmth, purpose, generosity, the courage to be seen.

## Shadow
Pride. The need to be the centre. Burning out. A glare that hides more than it shows.

## Jungian echo
The Self as the centre of the psyche, and the solar hero Jung followed through *Symbols of Transformation*, who has to leave home, go under, and come back. In alchemy, gold and the *rubedo*, the reddening at the end of the work, when what has been purified comes back to life.

## Myths
- ra-night-boat: Ra sails the sun's boat through the underworld every night, fights the serpent Apep, and is reborn at dawn (Egyptian)
- amaterasu-cave: Amaterasu hides in a cave and the world goes dark, until a goddess dances so wildly that the gods' laughter draws her out (Japanese)
- phaethon: Phaethon begs to drive his father's sun chariot for a single day and can't hold the horses (Greek)
- maui-sun: Maui snares the sun with ropes and beats it until it agrees to cross the sky more slowly (Polynesian)

## Images
A sunflower turning. A lion asleep in the heat. A gold coin. A brass door knocker. A bonfire at midsummer. A cat in a patch of light. A crown of dry grass. Noon with no shadow.

## Readings

### engraver · synchronicity
The Sun is not a lamp. He is a lion who swallowed a furnace and cannot stop singing about it. Every morning he climbs the stair of the east as if no one had climbed it before; every evening he walks down into the dark without complaint. What is not spent is not had. The coin kept in the fist goes green. What in you is shining today whether or not anyone is watching? And what have you been carrying behind your hand, like a candle through wind?

### mythographer · imaginal
The Egyptians said the sun dies every evening. Ra boards his night boat and sails through the twelve hours of the underworld, and every night the great serpent rises to swallow him, and every night he comes up in the east, reborn. In Japan they tell of Amaterasu, who hid in a cave until laughter drew her out. In both stories the light doesn't simply shine. It goes under and has to be brought back. Where has your own light gone under lately, and what might coax it out?

### howler · trickster
Hail the Sun, show-off, gold-toothed, blazing on the bus stop and the car park and the dog asleep on the porch! Hail every ordinary thing lit up like a saint for thirty seconds at five o'clock! The Sun never asks if it deserves to shine, it just does it all day with its shirt off. Take a page and write down every time you shone a little, in the small ways: the good joke, the fixed hinge, the kind word. Brag on paper. Nobody's reading.

### nun-at-the-sea · synchronicity
There's a teaching that underneath all our confusion is something that was never damaged: a basic warmth and brightness, like the sun behind cloud. We spend a lot of energy trying to become worthy of it, as if it had to be earned. It doesn't. The clouds are real, and so is the sun. You don't have to manufacture your goodness today; you only have to stop hiding from it. Write down one moment when you glimpsed it lately, in yourself or someone else, and didn't look away.

### grief-walker · synchronicity
Now, every evening the sun goes down, and the old ones never took it for granted. They sang it under. They knew that light which never sets is a kind of tyranny, and that a day is precious because it ends. We've forgotten how to let things set. We keep the lamps on and the screens lit and call it living. Something in your life is going down into its evening. Let it. Write a few lines to sing it under, and notice what the dark lets you see.

### fairy-tale-reader · trickster
In the tales the king always has a golden ball, or a golden apple, or a golden bird, and it's always getting lost down a well or stolen from the garden at night. Nobody asks why the king can't hold onto his gold. I'll tell you why: gold wants to move. Your own light is like that. Hoard it, and some frog will fetch it back for a price. Where are you clutching your golden ball? And what did you dream?

## Figure prompts

### synchronicity
- The Sun is where you're most alive. Where did you feel it lately, even for a minute?
- What are you for, this season? Not your job; your purpose.
- Where are you shining for an audience, and where for its own sake?

### imaginal
- A king sits on a throne in the middle of your chest. What is he wearing, and is he happy up there?
- The Heart-Fire needs fuel. What feeds it, and what smothers it?
- Picture your life seen from above at noon, with no shadows. What stands out?

### trickster
- The dice came up Sun. Write a two-line speech accepting an award for something small you did well.
- Describe yourself in the third person, the way a generous friend would at a party.
- If you were a lamp, what kind would you be, and where would you stand?

## Wild cards
- What would you do if you were sure you'd be forgiven for shining too brightly?
- Name something you're proud of that you've never told anyone.
- Write the first line of your own myth, beginning "In the year the sun..."
- Draw the sun the way you drew it at six. Is it smiling?
- What's the difference between being seen and being watched?
- Who taught you to dim yourself, and did they mean to?
