# Neptune: the Sea-Dreamer, the Veiled One

## Core
Dream, dissolution, longing, the oceanic. What has no edges.

## Gift
Imagination, compassion, music, the feeling of belonging to everything.

## Shadow
Fog. Escape. Self-deception. The rescuer and the victim. A longing no person can satisfy.

## Jungian echo
The collective unconscious as an ocean. *Participation mystique*, which Jung borrowed from Lévy-Bruhl: the state where self and world are not yet separate. In alchemy, the *solutio*, dissolving in water, where hard things soften. Grof linked Neptune with the oceanic peace of the womb.

## Myths
- selkie: A seal-woman comes ashore, a fisherman hides her skin, and years later she finds it and goes back to the sea (Scottish and Irish)
- butterfly-dream: Zhuangzi dreams he's a butterfly and wakes unsure whether he's a man who dreamed of a butterfly or a butterfly dreaming it's a man (Chinese, Taoist)
- vishnu-ocean: Vishnu sleeps on the coils of a great serpent in the cosmic ocean, and the world is his dream (Indian, Hindu)
- oisin-tir-na-nog: Oisín rides to the Land of Youth for what seems three years, and comes home to find three hundred have passed (Irish)
- city-of-ys: Ys, the city built below the tide, drowns when the golden key to its sea gates is stolen, and its bells still ring under the bay (Breton)

## Images
Fog on a harbour. A tide-pool with an anemone. A glass float caught in a net. A cello note held long. Sea-glass. A lighthouse beam in mist. A reflection in still water. A dream you can't quite hold.

## Readings

### old-man-at-the-lake · synchronicity
In the evenings the lake goes flat and the mountains stand in it upside down, and for a while you cannot say which is the real one. In India they say Vishnu lies asleep on the cosmic ocean and dreams the world. I do not know if that is true. I know the unconscious is an ocean, the ego is a small boat on it, and the boat does well to remember the water. Neptune dissolves. Let him. Then write down what floated up.

### pond-walker · imaginal
Early fog on the pond, so thick the far bank was only a rumour. A swan came out of it, then went back in, and I wasn't sure I'd seen it. Neptune lives there, in the not-quite. He's the part of you that longs for something without a name, that cries at music, that forgives too easily. It's a gift. Then the question, which isn't soft: what have you been pretending not to see because the fog is so beautiful?

### tavern-mystic · trickster
Neptune never pays for his drinks; he just smiles, and you forget to ask. He'll tell you the sea is in your glass, and for a moment it is. I adore him. I also count the spoons when he leaves. Beloved, the longing in you is holy, and it has been trying to get satisfied at the wrong bar. No person, no purchase, no endless scroll will fill a thirst that's really for the ocean. Write down what you're thirsty for. Then have a glass of water.

### nun-at-the-sea · synchronicity
We have a lot of ways to numb out: the phone, the extra glass, the daydream, keeping busy. None of them are wicked. They're just ways of not being here, because here is sometimes uncomfortable. Neptune loves an exit. The practice is gentle: notice when you're reaching for one, and pause for one breath before you reach. You don't have to stop. You're only getting curious about what you're leaving. Write down one thing you reached for today, and what you felt just before.

### grief-walker · imaginal
Now, the sea takes things. Every coastal people knew it and made their peace with it, the way you'd make peace with a difficult god. Picture a woman on a stone pier at dusk throwing flowers onto the water for someone who didn't come home. She isn't asking for them back. She's keeping faith with the loss. Neptune asks that of us too: not to drown in longing, and not to deny it either. What have you given to the sea? Write it a few lines, and throw them in.

### fairy-tale-reader · trickster
In the selkie tales the fisherman hides the seal-skin in the rafters, and the woman keeps his house for years, and she's good at it, and she is never quite there. Then one of the children finds the skin. Nobody in the story is a villain. That's the trouble with Neptune: everyone is half-dreaming. So be practical for once. What's your seal-skin, the thing that would take you straight back to the water, and who hid it, you or someone else? Draw the rafters. And what did you dream?

## Figure prompts

### synchronicity
- What have you been longing for without being able to name it?
- Where are the edges blurring in your life right now, and is that a relief or a worry?
- Which dream or image has followed you through the week?

### imaginal
- You're standing at the edge of the sea at night. Something is calling from the water. What?
- The Veiled One lifts the veil for a moment. What do you see, and what does it cost to look?
- Describe the room where your deepest longing lives.

### trickster
- The dice came up Neptune. Write three lines with your eyes closed. Don't correct them.
- Describe your week as a painting that's been left out in the rain.
- Write a message in a bottle for whoever finds it in a hundred years.

## Wild cards
- What are you escaping into lately, and what is it helping you escape from?
- Write about a piece of music as if it were a place you once lived.
- Which of your illusions would you keep even knowing it was one?
- What do you forgive too easily, and in whom?
- If you could dissolve one boundary for a day, which one?
- Describe the colour of the inside of your eyelids right now.
