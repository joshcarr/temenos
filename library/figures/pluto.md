# Pluto: the Lord Below, the Keeper of Seeds

## Core
Depth, power, endings. What's buried, and what grows from it.

## Gift
Transformation, depth of feeling, the power to let die what must, and to begin again.

## Shadow
Control. Obsession. The grudge. Power held over others, or turned against yourself.

## Jungian echo
The shadow at its deepest layer, where the energy is. The death-and-rebirth pattern Jung traced through myth and dream. In alchemy, the *mortificatio* and *putrefactio*: rot as the start of new life. The Greeks also called Hades *Plouton*, the Rich One, because the wealth of the earth, its seeds and metals, lies below.

## Myths
- persephone-pomegranate: Persephone eats six pomegranate seeds in the underworld and must return there for part of every year (Greek)
- inanna-descent: Inanna goes down to her sister's underworld, gives up a garment at each of seven gates, and hangs three days on a hook before she returns (Sumerian)
- orpheus: Orpheus sings his way into the underworld to bring back Eurydice, and loses her by looking back (Greek)
- isis-osiris: Osiris is cut apart and scattered, and Isis searches the land for every piece and breathes him back to life (Egyptian)
- izanami: Izanagi follows his dead wife into the land of the dead and, against her wish, lights a torch to see her (Japanese)

## Images
A pomegranate split open. Compost steaming in cold air. A seed in the dark. A mine shaft. A snake shedding its skin. A locked cellar door. Obsidian. Roots cracking a paved road.

## Readings

### grief-walker · synchronicity
Now, the old ones knew that nothing grows that didn't first go down into the dark and rot a little. They knew it, and we've mostly forgotten, and we pay dearly for the forgetting. Pluto is the lord of that place below, and he isn't cruel. He's a steward. He keeps the seeds. Something in you is going under this season, or has already gone, and there's a grief in that which deserves honouring, not hurrying. What are you being asked to let end, with dignity? Write it the way you'd write a name on a stone.

### old-man-at-the-lake · imaginal
The Greeks called him Plouton, the rich one, because all the wealth is underground: the seeds, the silver, the dead. I have found this true of the psyche. What we bury we do not lose. It goes on living down there, and grows stronger, and one day it comes up through the floorboards. Better to go down willingly and see what you have been keeping. Take a lamp. Take something to write on. The Lord Below is not your enemy. Ask him what he is holding for you.

### fairy-tale-reader · trickster
Every fairy tale has a room you mustn't open. Bluebeard's little key, the thirteenth door, the cellar. And every heroine opens it, and it's always worse than she feared and better for her in the end. So let's not be precious. There's a door in your house you keep walking past. You know which one. You don't have to go in. Just stand outside it on the page and describe the handle. And what did you dream?

### nun-at-the-sea · imaginal
Sooner or later things come apart: a relationship, a plan, the picture of ourselves we worked so hard on. We tend to treat each collapse as a mistake, when it might be the most honest thing that has happened in years. Picture sitting in the rubble of something that ended, not rebuilding yet, just sitting in the dust with light coming through where the roof used to be. Pluto keeps that place. What came apart for you, lately or long ago? Write what you can see now that the walls are down.

### engraver · trickster
The Lord Below keeps a shop under the city, and everything you ever threw away is on his shelves: the tantrum, the teenage poem, the grudge in its tin, the love you swore you were over. He sells them back at a fair price. Nothing is lost in the cellar; it only ripens. Go down and browse. Pick one thing off the shelf you'd forgotten owning. Write what it cost you to bury it, and what it costs to buy it back.

### mythographer · synchronicity
Isis walked the whole of Egypt gathering the pieces of Osiris from the reeds. In Japan, Izanagi went down after his dead wife Izanami and lit a torch where he'd been told not to look. In Sumer, Inanna hung three days on a hook in her sister's hall. Every people seems to know that something has to go into the dark before it can be whole again, and that someone has to go after it. You may have met a small version of this lately: something lost, scattered or sent below. Which piece of it have you found so far, and who went looking?

## Figure prompts

### synchronicity
- What's ending in your life right now, even quietly?
- Where are you holding on too tightly, and what would happen if you loosened your grip by one finger?
- What has been composting in you, and what's starting to grow from it?

### imaginal
- A door in your cellar has been locked for years. The Lord Below hands you the key. Describe what you see before you go in.
- Persephone ate six seeds and had to return below each year. What have you taken in that ties you to a dark place, and what did it give you?
- The Keeper of Seeds shows you one seed. What will it grow into?

### trickster
- The dice came up Pluto. Write the obituary of a bad habit. Be generous to it.
- Draw up a will leaving your grudges to people who'd appreciate them.
- Describe your shadow as a houseguest who won't leave, and give it a room.

## Wild cards
- What power do you have that you're afraid to use?
- What would you be free of if you forgave one particular person, for your sake rather than theirs?
- What do you keep in the cellar of yourself, and why keep it?
- Write down one thing you're done with. Underline it twice.
- Where is your power leaking out through control?
- Who were you before the last big ending, and what did you keep?
